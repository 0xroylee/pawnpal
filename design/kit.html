01Principles

Simple and modern means fewer things on screen, each one big and obvious. Every screen should pass the test "could a 6-year-old use this with the sound on and without reading?"

Board firstThe board is the biggest thing on every game screen. Everything else is smaller and quieter.
One voicePip's bubble is the only place text appears during play. No toasts, no pop-ups.
Show, then sayHighlights and arrows point; the bubble explains in ≤ 2 short sentences, read aloud.
An edge means pressButtons and tiles sit on a solid edge and sink into it when tapped. Cards and panels have no edge. Kid buttons are 72px (about 2 cm).
Quiet coachPip speaks on mistakes only. Stars, badges and cheering wait for the end of the game.

02Color

A white page, white cards with a light grey line, one ink color, and saturated colors for anything a kid acts on. Blue always means "do this": the main button and the better-move arrow. Coral means "careful". Each of these colors comes in three tones: a fill, a darker edge for the 3D bottom, and a soft tint for backgrounds. Nothing relies on color alone: grades have an icon and a word, the better-move arrow is solid and the threat arrow is dashed.

Neutrals

Color families: fill, edge, soft

White on blue is 4.54:1, the floor for button labels. Every pair the app uses is checked by scripts/check-contrast.mjs. Duolingo's green (#58CC02) and blue (#1CB0F6) fail that check with white text, which is why these are darker.

03Type

Nunito (variable, OFL) everywhere a kid looks: rounded, open, friendly, with a large x-height. Body weight is 600 because 400 reads thin on tablets. The parent area uses the system font on purpose, so it looks like "settings", not "game".

Sizes shown are the younger band (6–8). Profiles aged 9+ get data-band="older": text one step smaller (body 18, coach 21), buttons 60px, board coordinates on, read-aloud off by default. Older kids call big-kid-sized UI "for babies" (NN/g).

04Space, shape, elevation

A 4px grid. Generous radii keep things soft. There are no blur shadows. Cards get a 2px line. Anything a kid can press also gets a solid 4px edge underneath and sinks into it when tapped, so an edge always means "you can press this". The board sits on its own green edge.

05Board

"Meadow" board (sage and cream) with the horsey pieces by cham and michael1241: hand-drawn characters with faces, the set kids love on Lichess. Our Pip-style friendly set and the classic cburnett set are options. Overlays: yellow tint for what just happened, dots for where you can go, a white-and-coral ring with a "!" badge for danger, blue tint for help, a solid blue arrow for a better move and a dashed orange arrow for a threat. Arrows get a white halo so they stay visible on both square colors, including for color-blind kids. The board aims for 600px (75px squares) and never drops under 512px.

Your turn. Last move in yellow, selected piece, legal-move dots, capture ring on e5.

06Pip the Pawn

A cream pawn with a coral scarf. Flat shapes, one ink outline, no gradients. Five expressions, each tied to an event. Generated by scripts/build-pip.mjs.

happyDefault. Home, reveal, idle.
thinkingHints, LLM rewording, grading over 400 ms.
surprisedNudge after a mistake or blunder.
cheeringGreat move, better retry, win, new badge.
encouragingRetry, after a loss, "Next time…".

07Components

Every component below is plain HTML + components.css. In the React app each becomes one component with the same class names.

Buttons

72px tall by default, 60px with pp-btn--sm, 88px for the hero Play. One primary (blue) button per view. Secondary is white with a grey line and edge. Reward (yellow) only on the result screen. Ghost for the "quiet" choice. Press = moves down 4px and the edge disappears. The solid edge is deliberate: flat buttons hide what kids can tap. Disabled buttons turn grey instead of fading, so they look switched off rather than broken. Labels stay in sentence case: young readers recognise words by their shape, and capitals hide it.

09Motion

Every animation explains something or rewards something. Nothing loops forever. Under reduced motion, all durations become 0 and pieces jump with the last-move highlight showing what changed.

10Voice and copy

Pip talks like a kind older sibling: short, concrete, about the board. Every line works read silently or aloud, and nothing important is only in audio.

RuleSayDon't say
Praise the move or the effort, never the child's intelligence (Mueller & Dweck 1998)"Nice find! That was the only good move.""You're so smart!"
Name pieces, not squares"Your knight can be captured now.""Nf3 hangs to Qxg5."
Kind about mistakes, never blaming"Uh-oh! Is your knight safe there?""Wrong move." · "Bad move."
Plain verbs on buttonsPlay · Try again · Show me · Give up · NicknameSubmit · Resign · Username
Two short sentences, max 25 words each"Pushing a pawn to the middle was safer."A paragraph about pawn structure
Rare, varied praise; never the same line twice in a row"Way to go!" · "Great find!" · "Nailed it."The same "Good job!" after every move

Duolingo Chess's most common complaint is its mascot repeating "the same 10 or so irrelevant phrases" on every puzzle; reviewers mute him. Pip stays silent on ordinary moves.

11Accessibility and kid-proofing

  • Kid buttons 72px (secondary 60px); parent controls ≥ 44px. Board squares ≥ 64px, 75px target.
  • Text ≥ 18px in kid screens; coach text 24px.
  • Kid buttons stay ≥ 32px above the bottom edge (accidental taps), and destructive "Give up" lives in the top bar behind a confirm.
  • Body text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 (checked by scripts/check-contrast.mjs).
  • Shape before color: solid vs dashed arrows with a white halo, dot vs ring for moves vs captures, a "!" badge for danger.
  • Every grade has an icon and a word, not just a color.
  • Selected tiles change line weight (2px to 3px) as well as color. Disabled buttons turn grey instead of fading.
  • Coach messages and opponent moves go to an aria-live region.
  • Every control except the board works with the keyboard (react-chessboard's own keyboard support is weak).
  • No time pressure: no clock, nudges never auto-close. Works fully with the sound off.
  • No free text for kids; grown-up actions sit behind the PIN.
  • touch-action: none on the board so dragging doesn't scroll the page.
